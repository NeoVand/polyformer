# R7 mapping: core number systems (child development)

**1. Objects and techniques.**
Objects: the *approximate number system* (ANS), an innate magnitude sense that tells 8 from 16 but not 8 from 9 (precision set by ratio: Weber's law); *parallel individuation*, exact tracking of at most 3–4 objects (subitizing); *knower levels*, children learning "one", "two", "three" singly, then the *cardinal principle* (the last count word names the set's size).
Techniques: the Give-a-Number task (Wynn 1990); *signature limits* (ratio-graded errors mark the ANS, a cliff above 3–4 marks individuation: Feigenson & Carey 2003); Carey's *Quinian bootstrapping* (2009) and the Rips, Asmuth & Bloomfield (2006) objection that finite experience cannot fix "every n has a successor". Frank et al. 2008: Pirahã speakers, without number words, match visible sets exactly one-to-one but fail once one is hidden.

**2. Map to the problem.**
- P1: number-cognition researchers need a learner whose generalisation is proved; Give-a-Number tests small n, and Davidson, Eng & Barner (2012) found cardinal-principle knowers failing "one more" at larger n.
- P2: the for-all-n claim itself.
- P3: inside the learner; a child model calling a calculator models nothing.
- P4: state = count word plus counted/uncounted partition; rule = tag, say next word, move; check = the finite count-list transitions (29→30).
- P5: the ANS flags implausible exact answers; a head-vs-sheet gap beyond the Weber band blames the program.
- P6: cross-language count lists with child data (Miller et al. 1995: Mandarin speakers count higher earlier).
- P7: the field trusts behavioural signatures, not proofs; pair the certificate with a flat size × ratio error plot.

**3. Map from the problem.**
Our bounded-view theorem answers Rips: finite experience fixes an unbounded principle when the counting step reads a bounded view; mastering the finite transition set is the proof. The innate part is the step's locality, not a successor axiom. It recasts Frank et al.: one-to-one matching is bounded-view work on an external sheet; number words are the token that keeps exactness once the sheet is gone.

**4. Ideas.**
1. *Certified count lists across languages.* Train the adapter to emit the next count word from a view of the current word's parts, in English, Mandarin, French, German, Welsh. Certification proves counting to every n; rank languages by view-set size and examples-to-certification against published child orderings. Cares: the bootstrapping debate. Breaks: "ML venue", "product is the computation". Kill: Spearman below 0.5 between model and child rankings.
2. *Signature audit.* Plot a model's numeric errors over a size × ratio grid; exact procedures show no Weber gradient and no size cliff. Cares: auditors who cannot read Lean. Kill: an uncertified chain-of-thought model is as flat.
3. *Violation-of-expectation blame.* When head estimate and sheet answer differ beyond the Weber band, rewrite the program. A calculator gives the same blame. Kill: recovers under 3 of the 5.8 lost GSM8K points at 0.8B.

**5. Grade.** Idea 1, FRAME: it imports a debate, an audience and human data, not a mechanism; it serves science, not the product.
