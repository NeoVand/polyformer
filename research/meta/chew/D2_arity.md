**The Arity of Thought: Predicting Relational Errors and Their Rescue**

**Verdict: conditional go for a computational-cognition paper; no-go for claiming attention theory explains “four.”** Arity means how many arguments a relation combines in one processing step. It matters because six visible objects can require several easy comparisons or one difficult integration. The strongest opportunity is predicting **specific errors and selective recovery**, beyond accounts based on feature confusion, learned representations, and sequential computation.

**What the audit permits.** [AUDIT §§2, 3, 5](/Users/neo/repos/polyformer/research/meta/survive/AUDIT.md) establishes equal class-conditional **means** for additive lower-order scores on its specified distribution—not identical distributions or classification impossibility. The coupling result constrains fixed-representation attention logits: scores before probability normalization. Contextual representations, nonlinear readouts, and additional layers can escape. Cubic terms and Newton power sums supply particular solutions; neither implies a biological four-argument ceiling.

**Prior art that materially limits novelty**

- [**Halford, Wilson & Phillips (1998, Behavioral and Brain Sciences)**](https://staff.aist.go.jp/steven.phillips/papers/relational_complexity.pdf) defined complexity through the hardest step of the actual strategy, allowing chunking and sequential decomposition; they already proposed tensor and timing-based mechanisms. [**Halford, Baker, McCredden & Bain (2005, Psychological Science 16:70–76)**](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8070322_How_Many_Variables_Can_Humans_Process) found declining graph-interaction performance; the five-way result was 12/22 correct on one problem per participant, not a universal bound.
- [**Andrews, Birney & Halford (2006, Memory & Cognition 34:1325–1340)**](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/BF03193275.pdf) found relational-processing measures predicted relative-clause comprehension beyond simpler comprehension, whereas memory-span measures did not survive that control.
- [**Smolensky (1990, Artificial Intelligence 46:159–216)**](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/Smolensky-90-AI-Tensor-Product-Variable-Binding-and-the-Representation-of-Symbolic-Structures-in-Connectionist-Systems.pdf) introduced tensor-product binding: multiplying role and content vectors to preserve who occupies which role. [**Hummel & Holyoak (2003, Psychological Review 110:220–264)**](https://reasoninglab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/273/2021/04/Hummel-Holyoak.2003.pdf) used synchronized activity in LISA to bind roles, support analogy, and impose capacity constraints. [**Doumas, Hummel & Sandhofer (2008, Psychological Review 115:1–43)**](https://labs.psychology.illinois.edu/~jehummel/pubs/DORA_PR_2008.pdf) introduced DORA, learning relational representations through comparison. Neural implementation and representation-dependent rescue are established territory.
- [**Webb, Frankland et al., including Cohen (2024, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 28:829–843; arXiv:2309.06629)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.06629v3) synthesized the relational bottleneck: restricting a pathway to relationships rather than object details improves abstraction. This restricts information content, not arity. [**Webb, Sinha & Cohen (2021, ICLR; arXiv:2012.14601)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.14601) demonstrated novel-object generalization through recurrent memory binding. [**Kerg et al. (2022, arXiv:2206.05056)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.05056) obtained strong transfer with CoRelNet’s simple similarity representations.
- [**Altabaa, Webb, Cohen & Lafferty (2024, ICLR; arXiv:2304.00195)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.00195) introduced Abstractors, which extract relations into separate symbolic representations, including successful SET classification. Pairwise relation extraction followed by nonlinear processing already handles some apparently higher-order tasks.
- [**Webb, Holyoak & Lu (2023, Nature Human Behaviour 7:1526–1541; arXiv:2212.09196)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.09196v2) compared GPT-3 and humans on matrices, letter strings, verbal analogies, and stories. Their public repository contains human results. Crucially, several independent matrix rules do not automatically constitute a relation of that arity.
- [**Fesser, Ektefaie, Fang, Kakade & Zitnik (2026, ICML; arXiv:2604.12176)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12176) introduced REL and found increasing-arity failures despite fixed entity count and additional inference compute. This largely preempts “LLMs have relational-complexity limits.” [**Shang, Kreiman & Sompolinsky (2026, Scientific Reports 16:22146; arXiv:2502.17382)**](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13369344/) related representation geometry to transfer and human difficulty in simplified matrices. Human–model difficulty correlation alone is also insufficient novelty.
- [**Campbell et al. (2024, NeurIPS; arXiv:2411.00238)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00238) linked visual-language reasoning failures to feature interference and demonstrated rescue by reducing it. [**Nurisso et al. (2025, arXiv:2506.14797)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.14797) derived generalization–identification tradeoffs from limited representational resolution. These are mandatory alternatives to an arity account.
- [**Sanford, Hsu & Telgarsky (2023, NeurIPS; arXiv:2306.02896)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.02896) proved restricted shallow-transformer limitations and higher-order separations. [**Kozachinskiy et al. (2025, NeurIPS; arXiv:2501.19215)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19215) strengthened one-layer limitations and introduced Strassen attention. [**Chakrabarti, Pitassi & Alman (2026, arXiv:2602.02422)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02422) generalized higher-order attention and its expressivity/computation tradeoffs. A generic attention-arity hierarchy is occupied territory.
- [**Gong & Zhang (2024, arXiv:2409.10715)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10715) associated toy-transformer working-memory failures with dispersed attention; additional layers/heads substantially relieved them. [**Yang et al. (2025, arXiv:2502.20332)**](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.20332) identified successive abstraction, induction, and retrieval mechanisms inside ordinary LLMs. Both caution against equating pairwise primitives with pairwise effective computation.
- [**Hearne, Robinson, Cocchi & Ito (2025, bioRxiv:10.1101/2025.10.29.685457)**](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.29.685457) report transformer–human neural alignment during Latin-square reasoning. Abstract verified; full text and reusable data **unverified**. This remains an important novelty check.

**Strongest version: an exact prediction, followed by a causal intervention.**

For four available candidate pairs, assume a single choice stage with fixed encodings:

\[
L(q,j,k)=a(q,j)+b(q,k)+c(j,k),\qquad P(j,k\mid q)\propto e^{L(q,j,k)}.
\]

The audit implies

\[
C(q)=\log\frac{P(1,1\mid q)P(2,2\mid q)}
{P(1,2\mid q)P(2,1\mid q)}
\]

cannot change with rule cue \(q\): all cue-dependent pairwise terms cancel. A genuine three-way term can change it.

This ratio measures whether the cue changes how two candidate components combine. It supplies an exact error-pattern restriction, stronger than predicting lower accuracy. Extend through alternating contrasts over more roles. The cancellation itself is standard interaction algebra; the prospective contribution is successfully predicting behavior and interventions.

**Assumptions we would add:** fixed encodings, this probability rule, and limited processing time. Strategy mixtures or contextualization can violate the restriction without primitive higher-order attention. Behavioral success identifies an effective interaction, not cubic neurons. Even determining whether six binary inputs contain an odd number of ones permits repeated two-input updates. Nothing selects four without independently constrained resource assumptions.

**Decisive test.** Generate tasks with 3–6 interacting roles, six displayed entities, constant answer count, independently varied feature similarity, and matched lower-order statistics. Respect the audit’s sampling assumptions; distinct-card SET tables do not inherit its blindness theorem. Include cue-controlled pair selection and a second task family. Count the cue explicitly; rule counts cannot substitute for arity.

Compare single-stage pairwise, recurrent/deeper pairwise, Abstractor, genuine joint-term, and higher-order-value models under matched training, parameter, and compute budgets. A value model computes joint features before selection; it tests whether improvement requires higher-order routing specifically. Include interference and explicit sequential-rule accounts. Widening the same additive score cannot remove the probability restriction; changing representations through recurrence can.

Register the human prediction before collecting data: under deadlines, errors exhibit the missing interaction; extra time or a supplied intermediate relation selectively restores it. Similarity reduction should chiefly repair interference errors. Calibrate response noise and processing time separately, then predict held-out choices and response times. Require better prediction of actual choices than competing models, not another matching accuracy curve. Compare intermediate-relation support with a matched visual/memory aid that preserves integration demand.

**Existing data and cost.** Begin with [Webb’s public human results](https://github.com/taylorwwebb/emergent_analogies_LLM) and REL for model evaluation. Recode actual strategies, rule length, and similarity before asking whether arity adds explanatory power. Published Halford/Andrews results offer external checks, but raw trials are **unverified**. [SimplifiedRPM’s code/data archive](https://github.com/ShaneShang/SimplifiedRPM-Visual-Reasoning) exists; human trial availability is **unverified**. None verified supplies the complete choice/deadline design above. Existing data can reject the proposal cheaply; they cannot establish the proposed mechanism by themselves.

Estimate one week and **$200–500** for the model/data rejection test. If predictions separate, simulate statistical power, then approximately **120–160 adults × 20 minutes** costs **$800–1,200**, including platform overhead. Allow **4–6 weeks and $1,500–3,000 overall**, excluding salary and ethics delays.

**Kill:** stop before recruiting if recurrent pairwise/Abstractor or interference/rule-length accounts make indistinguishable predictions. Reject the human claim if held-out choices show no incremental predictive improvement or selective intervention effect.
