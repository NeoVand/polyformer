1. **What does language comprehension preserve: words, or distinctions needed later?**

   **Old question and data.** Why are nested sentences difficult when their words are predictable? Use StructuralCost’s 475 readers and working-memory scores, with Natural Stories for independent replication. *Nerode equivalence* groups histories producing identical outputs under every allowed continuation. It supplies precise controls for what memory compression loses within a specified grammar.

   **Decisive analysis.** Fine-tune matched small language models under equal memory budgets: retain recent words, selectively retain words, or retain unfinished grammatical obligations. Derive the required distinctions from finite grammars. Freeze predictions before testing held-out structural conditions and participants. Require correct interactions between sentence structure and memory capacity, plus transfer to natural text, beyond differences in word predictability. Remove and restore the proposed memory content inside models.

   **Prior art.** [Nusbaumer et al. (2026, Findings of EMNLP)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1360.pdf) released StructuralCost and documented models’ underestimated integration costs. [Futrell et al. (2021, *Language Resources and Evaluation*)](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~rfutrell/papers/futrell2021natural.pdf) released Natural Stories’ reading times for unusual constructions. [Hahn, Futrell, Levy & Gibson (2022, *PNAS*)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9618130/) already explained recursive processing through optimized word retention. [Kajikawa, Ai, Kuribayashi & Wilcox (2026, arXiv:2609.32119)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32119v1) connected discourse tracking to reading-time prediction.

   **Review case.** Nature Human Behaviour/PNAS potential comes from identifying what memory preserves and predicting specific failures across archives. Assumption: the grammatical abstraction captures relevant human distinctions. Kill it if gains are only aggregate fit. StructuralCost lacks item-level comprehension accuracy; use reading times. Its paper reports public data, but download access remained **unverified** here.

2. **Which memory mechanisms do human choices actually require?**

   **Old question and data.** Can behavior distinguish gradual reward averaging from memory for particular events? Reuse Eckstein’s public reward-learning records—862 participants, 617,871 trials—and independent human datasets from Ji-An’s study.

   **Decisive analysis.** Fit neural and compact symbolic competitors, including LLM-proposed mechanisms. In bounded discrete tasks, certify which models give identical future choice probabilities and find continuations separating the others. Search for those diagnostic histories in held-out archived participants and tasks. Compare running reward estimates, latest-event memory and memory for global reward context. Distinguish mechanisms required by observations from interchangeable descriptions; quantify uncertainty and missing coverage.

   **Prior art.** [Eckstein, Summerfield, Daw & Miller (2026, *Nature Human Behaviour*)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13212159/) showed advantages from flexible memory variables beyond standard reward estimates. [Ji-An, Benna & Mattar (2025, *Nature*)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12390849/) extracted interpretable strategies from tiny recurrent networks. [Kasenberg et al. (2026, bioRxiv:2026.05.18.725921; preprint)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13228651/) already used LLM-generated programs to discover mechanisms and validate predictions through reanalysis. [Shalizi & Crutchfield (2001, *Journal of Statistical Physics*; arXiv:cond-mat/9907176)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9907176) established minimal predictive-state theory.

   **Review case.** The contribution must be a substantive correction to what human learning data identify, replicated across tasks. Adding certification to model discovery is insufficient. Assumption: archived trials contain enough separating cases; test this before training. Exact claims concern specified models, never the complete human mind.

3. **Do languages compensate across grammatical cues because comprehension has limited memory?**

   **Old question and data.** Linguistic typology compares grammatical systems across languages. Use Grambank and [Universal Dependencies corpora](https://universaldependencies.org/) to ask whether case marking, word-order flexibility and structural arrangement jointly determine recoverability of “who did what to whom.”

   **Decisive analysis.** Construct finite grammar families communicating identical messages. Count future-relevant distinctions needed to recover participant roles; compare these predictions with communication-efficiency models and models containing only pairwise feature relationships. Fine-tune matched LLMs on alternative grammars, with randomly initialized models controlling prior linguistic exposure. The decisive result is a predicted three-way compensation pattern, including exceptions, in language families excluded from fitting. Control ancestry, geography, annotation and missingness.

   **Prior art.** [Hahn, Jurafsky & Futrell (2020, *PNAS*)](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~rfutrell/papers/hahn2020universals.pdf) recovered word-order regularities through efficient-communication optimization. [Verkerk et al. (2025 online/2026 issue, *Nature Human Behaviour*)](https://sfb1102.uni-saarland.de/sfbunisb/uploads/2025/11/s41562-025-02325-z.pdf) tested 191 proposed universals across 2,430 Grambank languages while controlling ancestry and geography. [Li & Liu (2025, *Entropy* 27:1128)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12651683/) already examined morphology, order and directionality jointly.

   **Review case.** Nature Human Behaviour potential requires a new quantitative constraint explaining exceptions and unseen combinations. A correlation map or rediscovered case/order tradeoff will not suffice. Assumption: finite-fragment memory costs transfer to real grammars; rare combinations cannot simply be labelled impossible. This has broader reach than #1 but greater linguistic and statistical risk.

4. **When does learning a new representation make a difficult concept easy?**

   **Old question and data.** Category learning studies how people group examples. Reuse public [Psych-101 category trials](https://huggingface.co/datasets/marcelbinz/Psych-101), including Badham’s younger/older-adult experiments, plus published concept-difficulty aggregates. *Interaction order* means how many features jointly enter a relation in a specified representation. Changing that representation can change the order, making it a candidate explanation for learning transitions.

   **Decisive analysis.** Separate shortest-rule complexity, interaction order and similarity-based grouping on the actual archived stimuli. Compare matched ordinary-attention, higher-order-attention and learned-representation models. Certify finite stimulus calculations. Predict trial-by-trial confusions and learning transitions in held-out category families, especially where candidate explanations disagree. Test whether learned intermediate representations explain those changes.

   **Prior art.** [Feldman (2000, *Nature*)](https://courses.media.mit.edu/2003spring/mas963/feldman_nature.pdf) related concept difficulty to shortest Boolean descriptions. [Feldman (2006, *Journal of Mathematical Psychology* 50:339–368)](https://www.researchwithrutgers.org/en/publications/an-algebra-of-human-concept-learning/) already decomposed concepts by feature degree—an important omission from the first-pass memo; abstract verified, full text **unverified**. [Badham, Sanborn & Maylor (2017, *Psychology and Aging*)](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/88425/7/WRAP-deficits-category-older-rule-based-clustering-Maylor-2017.pdf) found larger age deficits for less complex categories, supporting a clustering explanation. [Binz et al. (2025, *Nature*)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12390832/) introduced Psych-101 and fine-tuned Centaur to predict behavior.

   **Review case.** A representation-change mechanism explaining difficult counterexamples could support Nature Human Behaviour/PNAS. “Interaction order predicts difficulty” is insufficiently novel. Assumption: archives contain enough contrasting learning trajectories. Split studies independently of Centaur’s training exposure. The audit’s equal-class-means result supplies no general impossibility theorem for ordinary attention.

5. **Does brain–language-model agreement depend on the computations comprehension needs?**

   **Old question and data.** Computational neuroscience asks whether similar responses imply shared computation. Reuse processed Narratives brain scans and Pereira’s sentence responses; neither requires recruitment.

   **Decisive analysis.** Build models with preserved or selectively erased memory for pending grammatical distinctions, matched on average language performance. Test whether those components specifically explain brain responses beyond word timing, position and simple semantic features. Hold out whole stimuli and participants where the design permits; replicate across datasets. Compare equally disruptive interventions to irrelevant components. Certification applies to finite model tasks; the brain comparison remains observational.

   **Prior art.** [Nastase et al. (2021, *Scientific Data* 8:250)](https://www.honeylab.org/pdfs/nastase_etal_narratives_2021.pdf) released Narratives: 345 participants listening to 27 stories. [Pereira et al. (2018, *Nature Communications* 9:963)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5840373/) demonstrated decoding of linguistic meaning from brain activity. [Hadidi et al. (2026, *Nature Communications* 17:5769)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13324000/) showed that methodological choices and overlooked stimulus variables can inflate apparent alignment. [Jia (2026, arXiv:2605.14025; preprint)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.14025v1) already proposed relational and mechanism-removal controls.

   **Review case.** Potential rests on a positive, selective computational explanation replicated across archives. Another alignment score or confound critique is crowded territory. Assumption: existing stimuli contain enough diagnostic contrasts. Model interventions establish causality inside models, not brains. This is the highest execution-risk option for a solo researcher.
