**The main danger is mistaking predictive success for scientific explanation.** For Neo’s language programme, a model-generated pattern becomes a discovery about humans only after independent human evidence excludes simpler explanations. The cases below distinguish retraction, correction, failed generalization and disputed interpretation.

1. **A-Lab: Nature corrected the discovery claim.** [Szymanski et al. (2023, *Nature*), *An autonomous laboratory…*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06734-w), combined machine learning and robotics to synthesize materials. Their [2026 *Nature* correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09992-y.pdf) clarified that “novel” meant new to the prediction platform, not necessarily science; manual reanalysis confirmed 36 of 40 successes, leaving four inconclusive, and removed one compound mistakenly included in training data. **Failure:** automated identification and ambiguous novelty inflated the headline. **Status:** corrected, not retracted.

2. **DeepCRISTL: evaluation failure caused retraction.** [Elkayam & Orenstein (2022, *Bioinformatics*), *DeepCRISTL*](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/38/Supplement_1/i161/6617528), used transferred neural-network learning to predict gene-editing efficiency. [Corsi, Anthon & Gorodkin (2023, *Bioinformatics*)](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btad327) challenged its evaluation and demanded genuinely independent test datasets. The [journal’s 2023 retraction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10329910/) says readers could no longer rely on the findings. **Failure:** apparent improvement without defensible external validation.

3. **Emergent abilities: scoring can manufacture sudden breakthroughs.** [Wei et al. (2022, *TMLR*, arXiv:2206.07682)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07682) described abilities appearing abruptly as models grow. [Schaeffer, Miranda & Koyejo (2023, *NeurIPS*, arXiv:2304.15004), *Are Emergent Abilities…a Mirage?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004), showed that all-or-nothing scoring can turn gradual improvement into apparent discontinuities. **Failure:** a property of measurement gets attributed to the model. This challenges particular evidence for emergence; it does not prove every abrupt capability change impossible.

4. **Brain alignment: simpler signals explain impressive correlations.** Brain alignment here means predicting measured brain activity from model representations; it matters because prediction is often interpreted as shared computation. [Hadidi, Feghhi, Song, Blank & Kao (2026, *Nature Communications*)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72253-7) reanalysed three influential datasets. Shuffled training/test divisions, representation extraction choices, word rate and position explained important apparent effects; simple signals fully accounted for untrained GPT-2’s predictivity. **Failure:** uncontrolled alternative causes. Their conclusions explicitly do **not** invalidate the entire field.

5. **Impossible languages: the comparison tested the wrong distinction.** [Kallini, Papadimitriou, Futrell, Mahowald & Potts (2024, *ACL*, Best Paper), *Mission: Impossible Language Models*](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.787/), found GPT-2 learned manipulated languages less readily than English. [Hunter (2025, *Computational Linguistics*)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.cl-2.7/) showed that a central comparison mixed up counting rules with distance between related words: adjacent versus nonadjacent dependencies cannot establish preference for human grammatical hierarchy. **Failure:** the control changes two things simultaneously. This is an interpretive challenge, not a demonstrated failure to reproduce the scores.

6. **Centaur: predicting choices does not establish a human simulator.** [Binz et al. (2025, *Nature*), *A foundation model to predict and capture human cognition*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09215-4), trained on over ten million choices across 160 experiments. [Namazova, Brondetta, Strittmatter, Nassar & Musslick (2025, arXiv:2508.07887), *Not Yet AlphaFold for the Mind*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07887), reported that generated behaviour systematically diverged from humans despite strong predictive scores. **Failure:** extending success at predicting the next response to simulating whole participants. The critique is a preprint; it does not erase Centaur’s predictive results.

7. **Theory of mind: passing stories leaves the mechanism unresolved.** Theory of mind means reasoning about others’ beliefs and intentions; benchmark success alone cannot establish that mechanism. [Kosinski (2024, *PNAS*), *Evaluating large language models in theory of mind tasks*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39471222/), tested eleven models on forty bespoke false-belief tasks. [Pang, Pang, Broeker & Hibble (2025, *PNAS*)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12280963/) argued that alternative explanations remained. Earlier, [Ullman (2023, arXiv:2302.08399)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08399) overturned apparent successes with small story alterations. **Status:** contested interpretation; Ullman predates the final PNAS paper and is not its direct failed replication.

**The review bar I would impose on Neo’s claim**—a recommendation, not universal journal requirements:

- Specify one falsifiable scientific claim and its strongest competing explanation.
- Preregister: publicly fix hypotheses, exclusions, sample size, scoring and stopping rules before confirmatory testing. This prevents exploratory choices masquerading as predictions.
- For human-language claims, obtain appropriately approved human data, unseen constructions and preferably another language; model agreement alone is insufficient.
- Match word frequency, meaning, dependency distance, training exposure and computational budget. Include simple competing methods.
- Repeat across model families and random initializations; reserve untouched datasets and seek an independent replication.
- Release runnable code, prompts, model versions, failures and uncertainty. Require interventions before claiming an internal causal mechanism.

[Nature Human Behaviour’s Registered Reports](https://www.nature.com/nathumbehav/submission-guidelines/registeredreports) offer review of methods **before** research, with publication provisionally accepted regardless of outcome.

**Solo feasibility needs correcting.** Lake & Baroni’s [2023 *Nature* paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06668-3) demonstrated human-like combination of learned components using neural training and human experiments: two authors, with Lake performing modelling and behavioural execution. Centaur had forty authors. Kosinski is a verified solo high-profile example, also illustrating scrutiny. A comparable solo *Nature* AI success is **unverified** here.

**Presubmission enquiries differ.** [Nature](https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/presub) accepts optional online enquiries: a referenced summary paragraph, references and broad-interest explanation; usually two working days, with no commitment to review. [NHB’s dedicated page](https://www.nature.com/nathumbehav/submission-guidelines/presubmission-enquiries) currently rejects research enquiries and requires full manuscripts. Its Registered Reports page contradictorily still invites enquiries; plan on a complete Stage-1 proposal.

**Assumption:** publication can be separated from the startup architecture. Computational cognitive science offers two defensible routes: a narrow human-confirmed discovery, postponing condition 10’s new architecture; or a model-only theorem, preserving engineering progress but restricting claims about humans. Neither supports promising Nature within condition 12’s one-week budget.
