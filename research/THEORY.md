# Composition, symmetry, and ordered Match-k

20 October 2026. Definitions and constructive results for `GROUPS.md`, checked against
`groups.py`, `holonomy_sectors`, `holo_mats`, and the Newton/holonomy branches of
`polybench.attention`. Numerical evidence: `results/theory_checks.json`, generated by
`test_theory.py`. Literature and attribution: [PRIOR_ART.md](PRIOR_ART.md).

**The defensible separation is between position-free commuting composition and
order-sensitive composition, not between all softmax networks and all holonomy
networks.** We prove a sharp information bound and a constructive, position-free
faithful-representation theorem for Match-3, the task actually measured. For arbitrary
Match-k we give a separate construction with position-selective gates. We do not
silently give those gates to the position-free experimental model.

Ordered subsequence products themselves are prior art: they are discrete signature
features, explicitly used in Seq2Tens. The results here concern their particular
matrix evaluation, marked-position readout, and group-relation task.

## 1. Definitions and the scope of the model

Let G be a finite group of order m, identity e, and multiplication written from left to
right in sequence order. A sample is X=(X_1,...,X_n), with independent uniform G-valued
entries. A marked position I is independent and uniform in {1,...,n}. For 2≤k≤n define

    Y_i(X) = 1{there is S={s_1<...<s_k}, i∈S, with X_s1 ... X_sk = e}.

The evaluation distribution is over (X,I); this is population token-balanced accuracy,
not the average of separately balanced accuracies for each sequence:

    BA(f) = (P(f=1 | Y=1) + P(f=0 | Y=0))/2.

Assume 0<π=P(Y=1)<1. The order-blind observation is

    O_i(X) = (X_i, multiset{X_j : j≠i}).

An order-blind predictor is any measurable function of O_i, with arbitrary additional
randomness independent of X. It is not restricted in width, computation, or readout.
It receives neither the index i nor token positions, a causal mask, neighborhood
features, or an order-sensitive preprocessing layer. A symmetric function of
**position-annotated** features is not order-blind in this sense.

For head h let U_h:G→O(d) be a token map and a_h:G→[0,1] a scalar gate. Write
M_j^h=a_h(X_j)U_h(X_j). The arity-r marked holonomy feature is

    F_r^h(i) = Σ_{S⊆[n], |S|=r, i∈S} ∏_{j∈S in increasing order} M_j^h.

Our F_r is entry `r-1` of `holonomy_sectors`. The code realizes U_h in SO(d) through
the exponential of a skew-symmetric matrix. It does not realize every O(d) matrix in
dimension d: reflections have determinant −1. A faithful orthogonal representation
ρ:G→O(d) embeds faithfully in SO(d+1) by ρ↦diag(ρ,detρ). A4 and A5 have faithful
representations already in SO(3), so no extra dimension is needed for these examples.

We allow a readout to retain the own-token identity and sufficiently many head
features before a pointwise MLP. An “exact representation of the task” means exactly
correct labels on the finite domain G^n×[n], not exact multiplication by a finite
ReLU/GELU network on a continuum. Given a finite feature set and a positive separation
margin, an MLP can classify it exactly: project distinct feature vectors generically
onto distinct scalars and interpolate the desired labels piecewise linearly (ReLU),
or uniformly approximate a continuous separating function closely enough (GELU).
We make no claim that the experimental width 64 or its fixed MLP suffices for the
worst-case constructions below.

## 2. A sharp order-blind Bayes bound

**Theorem 1 (order-blind ceiling).** Let p(o)=P(Y=1|O=o). Every order-blind predictor
satisfies

    BA(f) ≤ B*(G,n,k)
          = 1/2 Σ_o max{ P(O=o|Y=1), P(O=o|Y=0) }
          = 1/2 + E|p(O)−π| / [4π(1−π)].

Equality is attained by f*(o)=1{p(o)≥π}, with arbitrary tie-breaking. Equivalently
B*=(1+TV(P_{O|Y=1},P_{O|Y=0}))/2.

*Proof.* For a fixed observation o, predicting 1 contributes
P(O=o,Y=1)/(2π) and predicting 0 contributes P(O=o,Y=0)/(2(1−π)).
Choose the larger independently for every o. The comparison reduces to p(o)≥π.
Randomization forms a convex combination and cannot improve this choice. Using
max(a,b)=(a+b+|a−b|)/2 and summing gives the displayed identities. ∎

**Plain language.** A model that loses the order cannot recover it with a larger MLP.
The best it can do is choose the label most favorable to balanced accuracy among
all reorderings consistent with what it sees. Because positive and negative examples
need not be equally common, the correct posterior threshold is the positive rate,
not necessarily one half.

**Corollary 1.1 (commuting layers).** A position-free one-layer Newton model, or
holonomy model with pairwise commuting token matrices within each head, followed by
any pointwise readout, obeys Theorem 1. In particular this holds for the implemented
d=2 holonomy layer, because SO(2) is abelian.

*Proof.* Commutativity makes every subset product independent of its internal order.
Reindexing all positions except the marked token preserves the sums over subsets.
Gates, tokenwise normalization, arity mixing, a residual own feature, and a pointwise
MLP preserve dependence only on O. Newton features are precisely commutative scalar
products; the tested SO(2) matrix-to-complex identification is
R(θ)↔exp(iθ). ∎

**Plain language.** The dimension-two rotation model and Newton attention can exploit
which tokens occur and how often. Neither can tell whether two different tokens
were swapped. This is an architectural information limit for the stated
position-free setting, not a claim that these models achieve the ceiling in training.

### 2.1 Exact finite calculation and the reported numbers

There is an exact finite expression without estimating conditional probabilities.
For each observation o let A_o and B_o count positive and negative marked sequences.
Let A=ΣA_o and B=ΣB_o. Then

    B* = [Σ_o max{A_o B, B_o A}] / (2AB).

`test_theory.exact_order_blind_ceiling` enumerates all m^n sequences and all n marks,
groups them by own element and the sorted multiset of the others, and evaluates
this rational expression with integer arithmetic. For Match-3, the exact
cycle-counting method below also avoids enumerating all sequences.

For A4 there are 10,345,896 positive and 7,570,008 negative marks, so

**Proposition 1.2 (exact conditional posterior for Match-3).** Fix own element a
and counts c_x of the n−1 other tokens. Define the alphabet permutation

    f_a(x) = x^−1 a^−1.

In each cycle of f_a, keep the vertices with c_x>0. If a nontrivial cycle is fully
present, or a fixed point has count at least two, then p(a,c)=1. Otherwise the
remaining nonisolated components are directed paths. Include isolated vertices as
single-vertex paths and define

    D(a,c) = ∏_{paths P} [(Σ_{x∈P} c_x)! / ∏_{x∈P} c_x!].

Then the exact posterior is p(a,c)=1−1/D(a,c). A fixed point with count zero or one
adds no constraint. The probability mass of an observation (a,c) is
(n−1)!/[m^n ∏_x c_x!].

*Proof.* Cyclically rotate the entire marked sequence until a is first. The identity
predicate of every containing triple is unchanged, since its product undergoes a
cyclic rotation. The remaining ordered list is uniformly distributed among orders
with counts c. A positive triple is precisely a pair x before f_a(x), because
a x f_a(x)=e. Avoiding it requires **every** occurrence of f_a(x) to precede every
occurrence of x whenever both labels occur. A fully supported directed cycle makes
these constraints inconsistent; a fixed point needs two occurrences to form a pair.
For a path, the constraints impose one block ordering of its labels out of
(Σc_x)!/∏c_x! possible internal orders. Internal orders on disjoint label sets are
independent in a uniform multiset permutation: count their interleavings and their
internal words separately. Multiply the path probabilities. The observation mass
is its multinomial number of words divided by m^n. ∎

**Plain language.** Once the marked token is fixed, every other group element has
exactly one partner that would finish a successful triple if it appeared later.
Avoiding all solutions therefore imposes a small set of ordering constraints.
Counting the permitted orders gives the exact posterior; no random reordering is
needed.

**Exact aggregation.** Enumerate count vectors separately inside each cycle of
f_a, recording their total length and D (use D=0 as a sentinel for posterior 1).
A local count vector of total t represents t!/∏c_x! words. Combine disjoint cycles
by multiplying D values and word counts and multiplying by binom(t+s,s) for their
interleavings. Finally mix over a. This integer dynamic program produces the
complete posterior distribution, so grouping by D rather than by individual
observations leaves the Bayes optimum unchanged. It represents all
60^11=36,279,705,600,000,000,000 rooted words for A5 with just 140 posterior values.
The implementation checks conservation of word counts at every combination.
Its posteriors and posterior masses independently match **all 52,416 A4 observation
classes** from exhaustive enumeration. The method is exact; no claim of polynomial
complexity for arbitrary G and n is made.

### 2.2 Which triples actually depend on order?

**Proposition 2 (triple classification and a small-n closed form).** Among the six
permutations of three labeled occurrences (a,b,c), either zero, three, or six have
product e. If abc=e, all six have product e exactly when a and b commute,
equivalently when a,b,c lie together in an abelian subgroup. Otherwise exactly
the three cyclic rotations have product e. Thus a content triple is order-free
iff it has no successful ordering, or its elements commute and some ordering succeeds.
For n=k=3 and m>2,

    B*(G,3,3) = 1 − [1−cp(G)]/[2(m−1)],
    cp(G) = P(ab=ba) = number_of_conjugacy_classes(G)/m.

*Proof.* Cyclic rotations are conjugate: bca=a^−1(abc)a. The six permutations split
into the two cyclic orbits of abc and acb. If abc=e then c=(ab)^−1, and acb=e
iff ab=ba. In that case c commutes with both a and b. This also covers repeated
elements; if a repeated-element triple has an identity ordering, all its orders
succeed. For the formula, π=1/m and p(O) belongs to {0,1/2,1}. Because π<1/2,
Bayes predicts positive whenever any ordering succeeds. It has TPR 1. A uniformly
sampled positive triple is determined by uniform a,b and c=(ab)^−1; its
probability of being order-sensitive is 1−cp(G). The ambiguous-negative mass is
therefore (1−cp(G))/m, giving the stated false-positive rate after dividing by
1−1/m. Finally Σ_a |C_G(a)|=m times the number of conjugacy classes, by grouping
conjugate a, proves the expression for cp. ∎

**Plain language.** A successful triple remains successful when its entries are
rotated around a circle. Reversing their order can change the answer. If the three
operations commute, every ordering works; otherwise exactly half do. In longer
sequences many such candidate triples interact, so their individual ambiguity
does not directly give the experiment’s ceiling.

For n=3 the exact ceilings are 32/33 for A4 and 1405/1416 for A5. These are **not**
the n=6 and n=11 benchmark ceilings. The script exhaustively checks all 12^3 and
60^3 triples, including repetitions.

## 3. Faithful representations suffice, with the gates stated explicitly

A faithful representation is a homomorphism ρ with trivial kernel. For an orthogonal
ρ, define the strictly positive identity trace gap

    Δρ = d − max_{g≠e} tr ρ(g) > 0.

Indeed ||ρ(g)−I||_F²=2(d−trρ(g)); equality trρ(g)=d means ρ(g)=I and hence g=e.
This tests one product. It does **not** by itself test whether a sum of products has
an identity summand: a low trace from other summands can hide that summand. The
following constructions supply the missing selection or counting argument.

### 3.1 Arbitrary k with position-selective gates

**Theorem 3 (position-selective sufficiency).** Suppose gates can depend on position,
the readout knows the marked position, K≥k, and ρ:G→O(d) is faithful. One holonomy
layer with H=binom(n,k) heads of dimension d, retaining its arity-k features, and
a sufficiently wide MLP represents ordered Match-k exactly. For the implemented
SO(d) parameterization require ρ(G)⊆SO(d), or use the dimension d+1 embedding above.

*Proof.* Index heads by k-subsets S. Give head S the matrix ρ(X_j) at every token
and gate 1{j∈S}. At position i∈S its arity-k feature is exactly
ρ(∏_{j∈S}X_j); at other positions it is zero. Readout checks whether any head
containing i has trace d. Faithfulness and the positive trace gap separate the
labels.

There is also a finite-sigmoid implementation: use gate q∈(0,1) on S and
ε∈(0,q) off S. Divide the trace by q^k. At i∈S the unwanted terms have total
absolute trace at most

    d [binom(n−1,k−1)−1] ε/q.

Choose this less than Δρ/2. The threshold d−Δρ/2 still detects exactly the
identity product; ignore heads not containing i. Both gate logits are finite.
This gives strict classification margins, hence a pointwise MLP readout on the
finite domain. ∎

**Plain language.** Give each possible subset its own head. That head selects its
positions and multiplies their group matrices in sequence order. A faithful
representation returns the identity matrix only for a true solution. This is an
explicit existence proof with potentially many heads; it assumes positional gates
that the position-free experiment does not have.

The count H grows like n^k for fixed k. This theorem neither proves efficient
learning nor separates this augmented model from softmax supplied with positions.
Exact sigmoid gates 0 and 1 are not needed.
If the code's ε-RMS normalization is enabled, retain the full head matrices:
v↦v/sqrt(mean(v²)+ε) is injective for ε>0 and its inverse is continuous on
the finite image. A sufficiently wide MLP can therefore preserve this
construction's classification margin. This is a width-existence statement,
not a certificate for a compressed experimental projection.

### 3.2 The measured task: token-only gates suffice for Match-3

**Theorem 4 (position-free Match-3).** Let G have order m and a faithful orthogonal
representation of dimension d. For every fixed n≥3, a position-free holonomy layer
with token-only scalar gates, K=3, and at most

    H = 2 binom(m+2,2) = (m+1)(m+2)

dimension-d heads, followed by an MLP retaining the own-token identity, represents
ordered Match-3 exactly. All gate values can be chosen from {1/4,1/2,3/4}.
The SO(d) qualification in Theorem 3 applies here too.

*Proof.* For a vector t∈(0,1)^m, construct a head with M_j=t_{X_j}ρ(X_j) and
a paired head with M'_j=t_{X_j}I. At the marked token a=X_i divide their traces
by t_a (and by d for the identity head), obtaining the homogeneous quadratics

    Q_i(t) = Σ_{j<ℓ; j,ℓ≠i} t_Xj t_Xℓ tr ρ(product at sorted positions {i,j,ℓ}),
    N_i(t) = Σ_{j<ℓ; j,ℓ≠i} t_Xj t_Xℓ.

Let T_{i,bc} and N_{i,bc} be their coefficients for t_b t_c, b≤c. These are,
respectively, the sum of traces and the number of candidate triples with marked
element a and other-element multiset {b,c}. They can be recovered exactly by
finite differences. Set u=(1/4,...,1/4) and δ=1/4; evaluate at u, u+δe_b,
u+2δe_b, and u+δe_b+δe_c for b<c. There are
1+2m+binom(m,2)=binom(m+2,2) evaluation points. For either quadratic V,

    [t_b²]V = [V(u+2δe_b)−2V(u+δe_b)+V(u)]/(2δ²),
    [t_b t_c]V = [V(u+δe_b+δe_c)−V(u+δe_b)−V(u+δe_c)+V(u)]/δ².

Fix the multiset {a,b,c}. If none of its orderings has product e, its identity
count is zero. If every ordering does, its identity count is N_{i,bc}.
Otherwise Proposition 2 says that one cyclic orbit has identity product and
the other has nonidentity products conjugate to one another. Their traces share
a known value τ_{abc}<d. Writing q_{i,bc} for the number of identity products gives

    T_{i,bc} = d q_{i,bc} + τ_{abc}(N_{i,bc}−q_{i,bc}),
    q_{i,bc} = (T_{i,bc}−τ_{abc}N_{i,bc})/(d−τ_{abc}).

Thus all these nonnegative integer counts are determined by the head features and
own-token identity. The label is 1 iff their sum is positive. In the unnormalized
case a threshold 1/2 gives a uniform margin. Division by t_a only selects one of
three finite constants, conditional on the own token and head. The MLP existence
argument of §1 completes the construction. ∎

**Plain language.** For triples, we do not need to select individual positions.
Different token gates let us separate candidate triples by which elements they
contain, even when tokens repeat. Each such triple has only two possible
trace values across its orderings. An extra head counts how many candidates there
were, so the trace sum reveals exactly how many were successful. This supplies
the step that “a faithful representation recognizes identity” alone was missing.

**Normalization and projection.** The code divides arity 3 by binom(n−1,2) and
RMS-normalizes that entire arity across heads and matrix entries, using one common
positive factor at each position. Consequently all recovered T and N acquire the
same factor, and T−τN still detects q>0. Because n and the domain are finite and the
factor is bounded, a positive separating threshold exists. The script also checks
this normalized decoder directly. More generally the code’s ε=10^−6 radial
normalization is injective, though inversion is not needed for this theorem.
The shared output projection can retain each head’s trace (and an identity-head
diagonal entry), and `alpha` can zero all arities except 3. This requires
sufficient output width and does not claim that the trained width-64 projection
preserves every construction head.

**Implementation of the maps.** A token lookup of a skew logarithm realizes any
finite table of SO(d) matrices, since every rotation has a real skew-symmetric
logarithm. A sufficiently wide token embedding can store that table and the gate
logits. All gate logits here are finite. A BOS token can be assigned a small
positive gate: the finite label margin makes its effect harmless. The mathematical
definition omits BOS; the numerical construction checks the group-token kernel.

**What remains open.** We do not prove the same fixed-d, token-only theorem for
arbitrary k. At k≥4 there can be more than two conjugacy/trace types for the same
content multiset, so the counting argument above no longer applies. General
position-selective sufficiency is not a proof of that stronger assertion. Nor do
we prove the optimal number of heads, or convergence of gradient descent.

## 4. Necessity: what dimension and abelianization do and do not imply

**Proposition 5 (commutativity obstruction).** Suppose there exist a,b∈G with ab≠ba
but U_h(a)U_h(b)=U_h(b)U_h(a) in every head. Any token-only holonomy layer, with
arbitrary scalar token gates and any pointwise readout retaining only the own token,
cannot solve Match-3 on every input of any fixed length n≥3.

*Proof.* Set c=(ab)^−1 and compare (a,b,c) with (b,a,c), marking the last position.
The first is positive and the second negative. Every marked arity agrees:
arity 1 is M_c; arity 2 is (M_a+M_b)M_c; arity 3 is M_aM_bM_c=M_bM_aM_c.
The own token is c in both examples. Hence all readout inputs agree.

For n>3 append n−3 identity elements in both examples, still marking c.
Here a,b,c are nonidentity, and neither ac nor bc nor their reversals is identity:
for example c=a^−1 would force b=e. The added tokens therefore produce no extra
identity triple containing c. Every marked subset containing both adjacent a,b
still has their matrices adjacent in its product, and subsets containing just one
are paired under the swap. Thus every arity remains identical. This completes
the extension to n≥3. ∎

**Plain language.** If every head makes two genuinely different-order operations
commute, swapping them produces a concrete pair of examples the model cannot
distinguish. No classifier after the layer repairs that collision. This proves
that some noncommutative information must survive, without assuming training
produced exact group representations.

**Corollary 5.1 (the exact homomorphic criterion for Match-3).** Fix a finite
collection of orthogonal representations ρ_1,...,ρ_L. Permit arbitrarily many
scalar-gated copies and an unrestricted readout retaining the own-token identity.
Exact Match-3 at every fixed n≥3 is possible if and only if the representations
jointly reflect commutativity:

    [ρ_ℓ(a),ρ_ℓ(b)]=I for every ℓ  implies  [a,b]=e.

Here [u,v]=uvu^−1v^−1. At most 2L binom(m+2,2) gated heads suffice.

*Proof.* Failure gives Proposition 5. Conversely apply the finite-difference
construction of Theorem 4 to each representation. For any content triple admitting
an identity ordering, either all orderings are identity (use its count), or the
two orientations differ by a nontrivial commutator. Some ρ_ℓ sees that commutator,
so the nonidentity orientation in that representation has trace strictly below d_ℓ.
Use that representation's trace and count coefficients to recover the successful
count. Selection of a representation depends only on the finite content multiset,
which is known to the constructed readout. ∎

**Plain language.** Faithfulness is stronger than this task needs. For triples,
the gates can handle which elements are present; the matrices only need to
distinguish the orders that actually change the answer. Several representations
can share that responsibility.

For a concrete counterexample to minimum-faithful-dimension necessity, let
G=A5×Z3 and use its projection onto a faithful A5 rotation representation in SO(3).
Its kernel is the central Z3, so it is not faithful, but it reflects commutativity
because the discarded factor is abelian. Corollary 5.1 solves Match-3 nevertheless.
There is no faithful real orthogonal representation of G of dimension ≤3:
its restriction to A5 would have to be a nontrivial irreducible 3, whose real
commutant consists of scalars; an orthogonal scalar of order 3 is the identity.
The same absence in smaller dimensions follows from the A5 obstruction.

For **exact homomorphic channels**, every map to an abelian matrix group factors
through the abelianization G/[G,G], because each commutator maps to identity.
A4 has [A4,A4]=V4 and abelianization Z3; A5 is perfect, so its abelianization is
trivial. Thus exact A4→SO(2) maps contain only the trivial character or the
conjugate Z3 characters 1′,1″; every A5→SO(2) homomorphism is trivial.
Also A5 has no faithful real orthogonal representation of dimension ≤2:
a nontrivial representation of the simple group A5 would be faithful, its
determinant must be trivial, and SO(2) is abelian.

The A4 experiment detects the nontrivial quotient characters in **two of three**
d=2 seeds under its approximate homomorphism diagnostic; the other seed has none
below the error threshold. The theorem does not say that every learned head,
gate, or residual feature is a character. Token-dependent gates can distinguish
elements in the same quotient coset. Quotient factorization of an entire model
requires its gates and other features to factor as well. The unconditional
d=2 statement is commutativity and order blindness.

**Absence of a faithful individual head is not a general impossibility theorem.**
The intersection of several representation kernels can be trivial even when each
kernel is nontrivial: different sign characters of V4 give a simple example.
Moreover learned maps U_h need not be homomorphisms at all. Conversely, with
positions and unrestricted width, even commuting scalar subset features can
encode much more than their position-free analogues. Therefore we do not assert
“minimum faithful representation dimension = minimum neural-network dimension.”
Corollary 5.1 gives an even stronger counterexample with a single nonfaithful
matrix family. For A5 at d=2 the architecture itself is commuting; that obstruction
is sound.

The regular-character identity

    δ_e(g) = (1/m) Σ_{irreps ρ} dim(ρ) tr ρ(g)

is a linear Fourier reconstruction. It does not bound what a nonlinear readout
of gated faithful representations can do. Reachable Plancherel mass is a useful
representation diagnostic, not a Bayes accuracy bound or a necessary capacity law.
For general groups, the existing `reachable` helper also needs real/complex/
quaternionic type and determinant checks; its simple rule is only used here for
the listed cyclic, A4, and A5 representations.

## 5. Softmax: representation is not the measured learning outcome

**Theorem 6 (uniform softmax suffices for abelian Match-k at fixed n).**
Let G be finite abelian. One uniform-attention head carrying every character value,
an own-token residual, and a sufficiently wide MLP can classify Match-k exactly
at any fixed n and k≤n.

*Proof.* Set all attention logits to zero. Its output supplies every character sum
P_1(χ)=Σ_j χ(X_j), up to the known factor n. Since χ^r is another character,
the same value bank supplies P_r(χ)=Σ_j χ(X_j)^r for all r. Subtract the own
term to get leave-one-out power sums. Newton’s identities compute

    E_0=1,
    ℓ E_ℓ = Σ_{r=1}^ℓ (−1)^(r−1) E_{ℓ−r}
                        [P_r(χ)−χ(X_i)^r].

Character orthogonality then gives the exact number of identity k-subsets
containing i:

    C_i = (1/m) Σ_χ χ(X_i) E_{k−1}(χ(X_{−i})).

This is a real nonnegative integer, although individual terms may be complex.
The MLP need only classify a finite set of feature vectors by C_i>0; it can
approximate the polynomial computation within less than 1/2, which gives exact
labels. ∎

**Plain language.** Uniform attention can collect all the ingredients of the Newton
calculation. A sufficiently large MLP can combine them into the answer. The
experiment therefore tests whether ordinary training discovers that computation,
not whether softmax is mathematically incapable of expressing it.

For Z3^4 the forty conjugate character pairs, together with the constant character,
give the full bank. The constant sum is known from n. In `GROUPS.md` experiment 1,
both softmax depths with frozen characters stayed at 50.0% on Match-4 in all three
seeds; the exploratory 20,000-step extension did too (`grp_e1.jsonl`,
`grp_e1_long.jsonl`). The label “composition is needed” describes that registered
learning result. It must not be rewritten as a representational lower bound.

**Proposition 7 (positions remove a bounded-length softmax impossibility).**
At fixed n and a finite vocabulary, one positional softmax layer with n heads,
sufficient value/output width, and a pointwise MLP can represent every sequence
labeling function.

*Proof.* Use token one-hot values and positional keys. Head h assigns logit α≠0
to position h and zero to others, independently of the query. The n attention
weight vectors form

    A = [(exp(α)−1)I + 11ᵀ]/[exp(α)+n−1],

which is invertible. A linear output projection therefore recovers the ordered
one-hot tokens from the n weighted averages. Together with the query’s own
position, these identify the entire marked sequence; finite-domain MLP
interpolation represents any desired labels. All logits are finite. ∎

**Plain language.** With enough heads and positions, one softmax layer can simply
read the whole sequence and let a large classifier solve the task. That may be
an inefficient and difficult solution to learn. It rules out an unrestricted
one-layer impossibility claim at the finite lengths used here.

**Conjecture, not theorem.** Native composition may reduce optimization or sample
complexity for these group tasks compared with a softmax-plus-MLP implementation
at comparable resources. The experiments support an advantage at their tested
widths and budgets, but do not identify a general optimization barrier or prove
sample-complexity bounds.

## 6. Word problems, circuit complexity, and matrix recurrence

For a fixed finite group G, its word problem asks whether the product of an
arbitrarily long input word equals e. Balanced multiplication gives an NC1 upper
bound. For a fixed nonsolvable finite group, in particular A5 or S5, the group
word problem is NC1-complete under the standard small reductions. Barrington’s
bounded-width permutation-program theorem is the central connection. Solvable
finite-group word problems are in ACC0, hence in TC0; A4 is solvable. This is a
statement about growing input length and circuit families, not about how hard
a fixed short training problem looks. See [Barrington (1989)](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~barring/publications/bwbp.pdf)
and [Barrington and Thérien (1988)](https://doi.org/10.1145/48014.63138).

Applicable fixed-depth, polynomial-resource, log-precision transformer models have
TC0 upper bounds. Under the **unproved** assumption TC0≠NC1, those models cannot
solve all lengths of an NC1-complete word problem. The arithmetic/precision,
uniformity, depth, and resource assumptions matter; this is not an unconditional
claim about every real-valued softmax architecture. [Merrill and Sabharwal,
*The Parallelism Tradeoff*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00729) and
[*A Logic for Expressing Log-Precision Transformers*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02671).

Transformers can simulate general finite automata with logarithmic depth, and
special automaton/group structure permits shallower constructions; see
[Liu et al., *Transformers Learn Shortcuts to Automata*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10749).
Thus neither “transformers cannot track state” nor “softmax composes nothing” is
a literal representational statement.

The transition class is decisive for RNN/SSM comparisons. A linear recurrence

    H_t = H_{t−1} ρ(X_t),  H_0=I

stores the full ordered product exactly. Left multiplication stores the reverse
convention unless representations or input order are adjusted. Orthogonal/unitary
RNNs and modern input-dependent matrix recurrences precede this project.
The TC0 limitations in [Merrill, Petty, and Sabharwal,
*The Illusion of State in State-Space Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819)
cover the transition classes analyzed there, including diagonal selective
transitions; they do not ban unrestricted noncommuting matrix recurrences.
Negative-eigenvalue and products-of-reflections constructions further delineate
which group transitions such architectures can realize; see the verified entries
in `PRIOR_ART.md`.

Holonomy sits in this existing algebraic framework. Define the formal matrix
polynomial

    Z_t(z) = ∏_{j=1}^t (I+zM_j) = Σ_r z^r E_r(t).

Then E_r(t)=E_r(t−1)+E_{r−1}(t−1)M_t and E_t(t)=M_1...M_t.
The **full-arity sector of a prefix** is therefore its running product.
For a mark i, coefficient extraction gives exactly

    F_r(i) = [z^r] (∏_{j<i}(I+zM_j)) (zM_i) (∏_{j>i}(I+zM_j)).

This is also the marked-factor derivative
∂/∂λ_i [z^r]∏_j(I+zλ_jM_j) at λ=1.
The prefix/suffix dynamic program computes the marks without enumerating subsets;
the present code costs O(n K² H d³), not a constant-time oracle.
At full arity K grows with n. Fixed K=3 Match-3 does not inherit NC1-completeness
from the unbounded word problem. Sequential execution of this layer, or a
parallel product tree of logarithmic depth, is fully consistent with the circuit
results.

**Plain language.** A matrix RNN carries one running product. Holonomy retains
products of many subset sizes and reads the subsets containing each token.
The running product is already one of those sizes. That relationship explains
both the useful extension and the substantial prior art; it does not grant a
constant-depth transformer a free solution to arbitrarily long hard word problems.

## 7. Claims suitable for a paper

1. The sharp order-blind bound, exact conditional-ordering formula and A4/A5
   ceilings, and exhaustive triple analysis.
2. A constructive position-free faithful-representation sufficiency theorem for
   Match-3, with explicit positive gates, head count, and normalization treatment.
3. A separately qualified position-selective construction for arbitrary Match-k,
   plus the necessary-and-sufficient joint-commutativity-reflection criterion for
   homomorphic Match-3 channels and a counterexample to faithful-dimension necessity.
4. The empirical gap between learning native composition and learning its readout
   from complete character information, with fixed-length softmax expressivity
   explicitly acknowledged.
5. The particular marked, arity-resolved learned matrix-signature layer and its
   controlled group experiments, positioned against Seq2Tens, signatures, matrix
   development, and matrix-state recurrent models.

Not established: a new general theory of ordered subsequence products; a universal
faithful-dimension capacity law; position-free faithful sufficiency for every k;
an unconditional softmax impossibility; a proven optimization barrier; or a
language-model advantage. These boundaries preserve the negative results in
`SEARCH.md` and `LOCAL.md`.
